Enterprise deals do not stall in meetings. They stall in the days between them.

Value selling
Andy Walters·July 28, 2026

Every enterprise seller has had the same quarter. The meetings go well. The buyer is engaged. The forecast holds. And the deal still takes nine months to close, because the work that moves it forward keeps landing in the gaps between conversations.

We looked at five enterprise instances of AgentPress over six months and tracked where the calendar actually went. The meetings were not the problem. The median deal spent 71% of its life waiting on something a human had to sit down and produce.

Where the time goes

The waiting is rarely dramatic. It is a follow-up that goes out four days late, a security questionnaire that sits with the wrong person, a business case that only gets built once the buyer asks twice.

Broken down across the deals we reviewed, the recurring gaps were consistent:

  • Prep for a discovery call written the morning of, or not at all
  • Post-call recaps sent late enough that the buyer had already moved on
  • Stakeholder maps that lived in one rep's head and left with them
  • Business cases assembled by the champion, at night, without the numbers

None of these lose a deal on their own. Together they add months.

The champion problem

The most expensive gap is the one you never see. Your champion walks into a finance review you are not invited to, carrying a value story they built themselves from a pricing page and a slide. They are defending your product with your worst available evidence.

CRM hygiene does not win deals. Understanding the buyer, the value, and what needs to happen next does.

What we changed

The fix was not more reminders. It was moving the work off the rep's queue entirely, so that the artifact exists before anyone asks for it. In practice that meant a specific sequence:

  1. Reconstruct the account from email, transcripts, and CRM the moment the opportunity is created
  2. Produce the prep brief and discovery questions the day before every call, unprompted
  3. Capture commitments, objections, and stakeholders from the transcript within minutes of hangup
  4. Build the value model from the buyer's own operating numbers, then keep it current
  5. Publish it all to a deal room the champion can forward without editing

The order matters. A business case built before you understand the buyer is a template, and buyers can tell.

What it looked like after

Across the same five instances, cycle time fell by a median of 38 days. The change was not that reps worked faster. It was that the deal stopped waiting on them. The artifacts arrived before the buyer thought to ask.

What to do on Monday

You do not need a platform to start closing the gaps. Pick the two artifacts your deals wait on most often, and make them exist by default rather than on request. For most teams that is the post-call recap and the business case.
